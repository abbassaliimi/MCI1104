The Nature of Time Series Data

Introduction: Up until now, datasets were likely "snapshots" in time. If you shuffled the rows of a housing price dataset, overall patterns wouldn't change. Time Series data breaks this rule completely: time is the primary axis, and chronological order is the most important feature.

Core Concept: Data points are recorded at specific, usually consistent time intervals
(e.g., daily stock prices, hourly temperature, monthly sales)
The i.i.d. Assumption Fails

Standard ML models (non time-series) assume data is independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.). In time series, today's value depends heavily on yesterday's (autocorrelation). Standard ML models fail if you ignore sequence.

Decomposition (Anatomy of a Series)

Decompose series into 3 core components: Trend (long-term direction), Seasonality (repeating short-term cycles), and Residual (random noise).

Topic 02 Estimated Time: 45 Mins

Stationary &
Non-Stationary Time Series

Core Concept: Predictive models hate moving targets. If properties change over time, models trained on the past fail in the future. This is why we need STATIONARITY.

Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) Test Statistical Hypothesis Test
  • H0: Null Hypothesis: Data is non-stationary (has a time-dependent structure).
  • H1: Alternate Hypothesis: Data is stationary.
💡 Rule of Thumb: If p-value < 0.05, reject $H_0$ and confirm stationarity!

Conceptual Bridge

How Do We Predict Trends?

🤔 "If models cannot handle non-stationary data, how can we ever predict trends or seasonality?"

We don't predict the moving trend directly! We strip out the trend and seasonality first, use the model to predict stable step-by-step changes, and then reconstruct the original trend and seasonal patterns back into the final forecast.

The 3-Step Stationary Pipeline

Step 1: Strip Transform Data

E.g. Remove trend
($y_t - y_{t-1}$) to isolate stationary noise with a stable mean.

Step 2: Fit Predict Changes

Train the model (ARIMA/ML) on stationary differences that follow consistent statistical rules.

Step 3: Reconstruct Invert Transform

Add the saved trend and seasonal structure back to return realistic, full-scale forecasts!

💡 Think of it like driving: you predict changes in speed (stationary changes) rather than total distance driven (non-stationary trend), then accumulate speed over time to track overall position.
Topic 03 Estimated Time: 45 Mins

Data Transformation

Core Concept: Real-world time series are rarely stationary out of the box. Before feeding data into stationary algorithms, we apply targeted mathematical transformations to neutralize trends, variance, and cycles.

3 Primary Transformation Techniques Toolkit
1. Differencing Target: Trend

Subtracts the previous value to stabilize a shifting mean:

$$y'_t = y_t - y_{t-1}$$

2. Log Transformation Target: Variance

Compresses exponential growth and stabilizes expanding swings using Natural Logarithm ($\ln(x)$):

$$y'_t = \ln(y_t)$$

3. Seasonal Differencing Target: Cycles

Subtracts values from the same period in the prior cycle ($m$).
E.g. $m=12$ for monthly data with yearly seasonality:

$$y'_t = y_t - y_{t-m}$$
